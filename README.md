KenjaWord is a full word processor for real document work: styles, lists, tables, headers and footers, footnotes, table of contents, citations (with Zotero), track changes, comments, mail merge, equations — and it reads and writes the same `.docx` files Microsoft Word does, so documents move freely between them.

> "Kenja" is Japanese for *sage* — the wise one. Building smarter software is the mission.

## Highlights

- **Word-compatible `.docx`** — open what people send you, save back what they can open. Also reads and writes `.doc`, RTF, ODT, HTML, Markdown and plain text, and exports PDF.
- **Native and fast** — built in Rust, not a web app in a frame. Instant launch, snappy typing, on macOS today; Windows, Linux, BSD and a web version follow the same codebase.
- **Private by default** — no accounts, no analytics, no telemetry. Your documents never leave your device. App Store privacy label: Data Not Collected.
- **AI on your terms** — the AI pane connects to *your* choice of model (any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, including a local one like Ollama), with your own key. Chat stays on device; nothing routes through us.
- **Citations with Zotero** — insert and refresh citations and bibliographies through the Zotero app on your machine; documents stay compatible with Word's Zotero plugin fields.
- **Real review workflow** — track changes, comments, compare, and the familiar ribbon, shortcuts and dialogs.

## FAQ

**Does KenjaWord open real Word documents?**
Yes — `.docx` is the native format, including styles, tables, fields, footnotes, tracked changes and more. Older `.doc` files are read and converted too.

**Do I need an account or subscription?**
No. KenjaWord collects nothing: no sign-in, no telemetry, no ads.

**What about AI?**
The AI pane is optional and bring-your-own-model: you point it at any OpenAI-compatible endpoint (a hosted provider or a local model server) with your own API key. Requests go straight from your device to that endpoint — never through us.

**Which platforms are supported?**
macOS (Mac App Store), with Windows, Linux, BSD and web builds on the same codebase.
