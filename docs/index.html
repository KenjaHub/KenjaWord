04

Questions, answered

Does KenjaWord open real Word documents?

Yes. .docx is the native format — styles, tables, fields, footnotes, equations, tracked changes and comments all survive the round trip. Older .doc files are read and converted, RTF, ODT, HTML and Markdown open too, and anything can be exported or printed to PDF.

Do I need an account or a subscription?

No. KenjaWord has no sign-in, no telemetry and no ads, and no account is needed for any feature — including AI, where you bring your own model endpoint and key.

What does the AI assistant see?

Only what it needs to answer you: an overview of the open document, the text relevant to your question, and the results of searches it runs in that document. Requests go straight from your Mac to the AI endpoint you configured — never through our servers — and chat sessions live in memory only: close the app and they're gone.

Which AI models can I use?

Any OpenAI-compatible endpoint: hosted providers such as OpenAI or DeepSeek, or a local server such as Ollama. You configure the endpoint, model and API key in the AI Models dialog, and you can save several profiles and switch between them.

Does it work with Zotero?

Yes. KenjaWord speaks Zotero's word-processor protocol: insert and refresh citations and bibliographies from the Zotero app on your machine. Citations are stored the same way Word's Zotero plugin stores them, so documents move freely between Word, LibreOffice and KenjaWord.

Which platforms are supported?

KenjaWord is coming to the Mac App Store. Windows, Linux, BSD and a web version are built from the same codebase and are on the way.
